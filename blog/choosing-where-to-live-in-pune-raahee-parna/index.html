Where to Live in Pune: A Practical Checklist and How Raahee Parna Compares

By Raahee Corp · 27 October 2026 · 3 min read

Pune is one of India's most liveable cities, with strong schools, a busy job market and a pleasant climate. Yet it is a big city, and picking the right part of it for your family can feel daunting. This guide gives you a simple checklist for deciding where to buy, then tests Raahee Parna in Dudulgaon, near Moshi, against it.

Start with how your week actually runs

The best area is the one that fits your routine. List the places your family visits most: offices, schools, hospitals, grandparents' homes and favourite markets. Paying a little more for a well connected area can save hours every week, while a quieter location can lift your quality of life if the commute is manageable. Be honest about which matters more to your household.

Pune is several markets, not one

Central neighbourhoods offer convenience and heritage, though they tend to cost more and feel crowded. IT-led areas such as Hinjewadi and Kharadi suit people in technology. The Pimpri Chinchwad (PCMC) belt, which includes Moshi, Charholi and Dudulgaon, gives more space and greenery along with access to industrial, educational and commercial hubs. Each has trade-offs, so match the area to your own priorities.

Your checklist

  • Budget: set a comfortable total, including stamp duty, registration and GST
  • Commute: test the real travel time to work at peak hours
  • Daily needs: schools, hospitals, markets and colleges within 15 minutes
  • Amenities: facilities your family will truly use
  • Legal and developer checks: MahaRERA registration and track record
  • Future needs: room for a growing family or a work-from-home space

Visit more than once

A single weekend visit can mislead. Go on a weekday morning to see the traffic, and again in the evening to feel the neighbourhood after work. Walk to the nearest shop, note the noise level and see how busy the access road gets. These small observations often decide whether a location suits you.

Mistakes to avoid

Plenty of buyers choose on price alone and ignore the daily commute, or get swayed by a long amenity list without asking who will maintain it. Some skip the legal checks altogether. Take your time, compare projects on the same footing, and remember that the cheapest option is not always the best value. If you can, talk to current residents and ask the developer for clear answers on timelines, charges and approvals.

Decide with confidence

Shortlist two or three projects, see each at different times of day, and compare carpet area, amenities and total cost side by side.
